2. BeatXP Marv Raze — Best for AMOLED Lovers

If vibrant colors and inky true blacks are your priority, the BeatXP Marv Raze delivers an authentic 1.96-inch AMOLED panel with Always-On Display (AOD) support. At under ₹1,900, having 60Hz refresh rate and 800+ nits peak brightness makes this screen look like it belongs on a ₹5,000 watch.

5 Critical Things to Check Before Buying a Smartwatch Under ₹2,000

  1. Display Panel & Brightness: Look for at least 500 nits brightness. Anything below 450 nits becomes completely unreadable under direct Indian sunlight.
  2. Single-Chip Bluetooth Calling: Older budget watches required pairing two separate Bluetooth connections (one for notifications, one for calling). Modern watches use Single-Chip BT 5.3 which pairs once and saves massive battery.
  3. Health Sensor Realism: Budget smartwatches use optical PPG sensors. While great for heart rate during cardio and resting rates, never rely on blood pressure or blood sugar readings from any sub-₹2,000 device for clinical decisions.
  4. Waterproof Certification: Insist on minimum IP68 rating so sweat during intense gym workouts or sudden monsoon downpours won't fry the motherboard.
  5. Companion App Privacy: Brands like Noise (NoiseFit app) and boAt (Crest app) have refined, ad-free mobile apps that don't spam notification bars with third-party ads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I reply to WhatsApp messages from a smartwatch under ₹2,000?

Most watches in this price category allow reading full WhatsApp messages and notifications, and some (like Noise and boAt) offer quick pre-set SMS replies. However, full on-screen keyboard typing is typically reserved for WearOS or Apple Watch models priced above ₹15,000.

How accurate is the step counter on budget watches?

During our 1,000-step treadmill benchmark test, the top 5 watches recorded between 965 and 1,020 steps (within a 3.5% margin of error), which is highly satisfactory for daily fitness tracking.
